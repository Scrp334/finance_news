I. RETURNS.
Definition of return
        -the sum of net income or capital gains generated by an investment expressed in relative terms.
         NB: Returns have commonly been expressed as percentages because percentages can be appliedin any amount.

Returns can be expressed in several forms:
a. Dividend- return gained by shareholders for the capital contribution in the business.
        - based on annual residual profit of the firm.
        - in percentage, dividend gains are called dividend yields expressed as the following below:
            FORMULAE -> Dividend Yield = (Dividend per share / Price per share ) * 100%   

b. Capital gain - returns related to increase in shareholder;s share price.
        - normally for measuring firm;s efficiency it;s provided in annual terms.
            FORMULAE -> Capital gain = ( ( P1 - P0) / P0 ) * 100%
            where: P1 is share price at given time.
                   P0 is initial share price.

c. Holding period returns - total return for holding an asset for a certain period of time.
        - In summary it include total retunrs(capital gains + net income).
        - It helps evaluate performance across time, calculate tax liabilities and compare investment opportunities.
            FORMULAE -> Holding period return = ( (Dividend per share + (P1 - P0)) / initialshare price ) * 100%

d. Interest rate of return - is a form of return earned on the loan amount entitled to the lender.
        -   FORMULAE -> Interest rate of return = (Interest paid / Loan Amount) * 100%

d. Required rate of return/Huddle rate - is the minimum return amount expected to beeraned by an investor. 
        - generally it depends on 
            (i). what an investor would earn in a risk-free investment i.e treasury bills and 
            (ii). a premium for bearing risk
             hypothetical formula -> required return = risk-free investment rate + risk-premium

e. Expected return - a return anticipated to be received in the future based on probability-weighted average of outcomes.
        - it is forward-looking and merely statistical.
        -   FORMULAE -> Expected return = R1P1 + R2P2 + .... + RnPn
            where: R is return on respective P(probability outcome)


II. RISK
Definition of risk.
    - refers to the uncertainity of the required return to occur as anticipated or,
    - probability of occurance of losses in relation to the expected return.
    - most common meansures of risk are variance and standard deviation.

a. Variance risk measure - measures return volatility by measuring the squared deviations of an asset return from its expected return.
    - simply put: Variance = ((R1 - ER)^2)P1 + ((R2 - ER)^2)P2 + ... + ((Rn - ER)^2)Pn 

b. Standard deviation risk measure - being the square root of variance, it is a statistical measure of the variability of a distribution of 
                                     return around its mean(expected return).
    
c. Coefficient of Variation - is a statistical measure of relative dispersion of returns around the expected return.
    - it assesses the amount of risk an investor takes for every unit of expected return.
    - Unlike standard deviation measuring absolute risk, this method provides a relative measure.
    - It helps compare unequal asset say different industries while showing a "risk to reward ratio"
    - A lower CV generally means more favourable risk-return tradeoff representing less volatility per unit of return.
     FORMULAE -> Coefficient of Variation =  (Standard deviation / Expected return ) * 100%

d. Covariance and Correlation.(Minimum Variance/Mean-Variance Ootimization).
    -It accounts that assets move together, meaning if two assets are both low-volatility but are 99% correlated, holding both doesn't
     actually lower your risk as much as holding two assets that move in opposite directions.
    i. Covariance formula = sum( ((Ra - ERa)*(Rb - ERb)) / n - 1)
    ii. Correlation formulae = Covariance[AB] / (standard deviation of a * standard deviation of b)


NB: The overall risks that investors face can be Systematic or Unsystematic risks.
f. Systematic risks/Market risks - represents how asset's returns are affected by Systematic factors like business cycle, government policy, interest rates

g. Unsystematic risks/Diversifiable risks  - are risks specific to a particular asset. 
    - Markowitz's portfolio pioneered the concept of holding a diversified portfolio.
    - Thus individuals can reduce such risk by holding large diversified securities known as a portfolio.


III. PORTFOLIO
Definition:  A portfolio - is a group of assets/securities such as stocks and bonds held by an investor.
    - The extent to which a portfolio reduces Unsystematic risk depends on the correlation between the assets return.


a. Portfolio Construction using Inverse volatility Weighing.
    - It is a risk based allocation strategy.
    - Risk parity(each asset contributes roughly equal risk in your portfolio)    
    - It invloves 3 steps
    i. Calculate individual volatility.(using standard deviation)
    ii. Find the Inverse.(we want a lower number "volatility" to result in a higher weight)
    iii. Normalize weights.(Ensuring total portfolio equals 100% or 1).

b. Portfolio Return.
    - This is the weighted average of the expected returns on the individual assets in the portfolio.
    - Obtained by considering the weights of the assets in the portfolio with respective expected retunrs.
    - The idea is that the portfolio will compensate for the risk across assets making it much safer.(Markowitz portfolio)
     FORMULAE = WaERa + WbERb + ... + WnERn
     where; Wa = Weight of Asset a in portfolio 
            ERa = Expected Return of Asset a.

c. Portfolio Risk.
    - This concept is given as the ratio of the covariance of the assets and their standard deviation.
    - Sometimes this is known as the standardized covariance measuring the comovement of assets from -1 to +1 scale.
     FORMUALE -> cov(RxRy) / stdxstdy
     where: cov(RxRy) = (Rx - ERx)(Ry - ERy)Pi
